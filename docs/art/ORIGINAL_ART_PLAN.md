# ENSO original art plan: replacing Mana Seed

**Owner:** Saffron (Art Director), with Quill (Technical Artist) · **Decision:** Tokelo (Creative Director), 2026-10-26:
ENSO drops Mana Seed art and makes its own original art. · **Status:** plan, no art yet.

Mana Seed stays in the game until each piece has an original replacement that passes review. The game must be
playable at every step. The licence and permission record (`assets/LICENSE-manaseed.txt`) is kept until the last
Mana Seed pixel has left the repository, and is then moved to `docs/art/history/`, not deleted.

## 0. Hard rule

**Mana Seed art is never used as input, reference image, style source, palette source, pose reference or
"in the style of" prompt for any AI or generation tool (Agent Sprite Forge included), even while we replace it.**
This is the artist's condition in `assets/LICENSE-manaseed.txt`, and it applies to every agent and human.

In practice:
- Never attach, crop, trace, describe pixel by pixel, or upload any file under `assets/sprites/manaseed.js`,
  `assets/src/tiles/manaseed.png`, `assets/packed/assets.js` (the `tiles/manaseed` entry) or screenshots that show them.
- Visual regression screenshots taken while Mana Seed is on screen stay in `qa/` for human comparison only and are
  never fed to a generator.
- Generation briefs are written from this document's style guide (words, numbers, our own palette file), never from
  a picture of the current game.
- Mana Seed frame layout (64×64 paper doll pages) is a **data contract** we keep for drop-in swaps; matching frame
  keys and anchors is allowed, copying silhouettes or poses from their frames is not. New poses are drawn from the
  combat timing table (§2.4), not from their sheets.

## 2. Style guide for the original art

### 2.1 Resolution
- **Tiles 16×16** (objects in 16 px multiples; trees and buildings may span several). Unchanged.
- **Characters: 64×64 frames, feet anchor (32, 43)**, the same as the Mana Seed doll, so the engine, hitboxes,
  layer order and camera need no change. The visible body is about 16 px wide and 32 px tall inside the frame (a
  two-tile-tall person); the empty margin holds weapon arcs and shields.
- Drawn at 1×, rendered nearest-neighbour at whole pixels (existing `ui/sprites.js` rule). No sub-pixel, no
  anti-aliased edges, no mixels.

### 2.2 Palette
- One project master palette, `assets/src/palette/enso-master.gpl` (+ `.png` swatch), **48 colours max**, authored by
  Saffron from ENSO's own references (`docs/art/CH1_VISUAL_DIRECTION.md`): ash, ember, bone, crow-black, moss, rust.
  Every exported pixel must be in it (Quill's checker enforces).
- Ramps of 4 per material (highlight, base, shade, deep shade), hue-shifted: shadows cooler/violet, highlights warmer.
- Recolourable layers (skin, hair, cloth, metal) are drawn on **fixed key ramps**, then recoloured at load by
  `paletteFor()`-style swaps. This replaces 56 hair sheets and 10 outfit sheets with one sheet per style.
- Reserved colours, never used in bodies or tiles: telegraph red (`#ff2010` family), parry/stun yellow
  (`#ffe040`), hurt-flash white, UI accent. This keeps combat signals unambiguous.

### 2.3 Outline and shading
- Characters and enemies: 1 px **coloured outline** (darkest ramp step of the adjacent material, near-black
  `#14121a` only on the outer silhouette's bottom/back). No black interior lines.
- Light from top-left, single source. Two shade steps on bodies, no dithering on characters; sparse ordered
  dithering allowed on large tiles (grass, stone) only.
- Tiles have no outline; objects that stand on tiles (trees, boulders, chests, furniture) get the silhouette
  outline and a separate soft shadow drawn by code (as today).
- Silhouette first: every pose must read as a solid fill at 1× before any interior detail is added.

### 2.4 Animation frames combat needs (per facing; 4 facings, `right` may be `{ mirror: 'left' }` for symmetric
layers, weapons and shields drawn per facing)

Movement is 4-direction art today (8-dir input snaps to the nearest of down/up/left/right); keep 4 facings.

| Pose keys (keep these names) | Frames | Used by (`ui/sprites.js` timings) |
|---|---|---|
| `stand` | 1 | `idle` (1000 ms) |
| `walk1-6` | 6 | `walk` (6 × 135 ms); `dodge` uses `walk2`, `walk5` |
| `ready1-4` | 4 | combat idle `ready` (4 × 200 ms); `heavy` and `hurt` start/end on `ready1` |
| `slash1-4` | 4 | `attack`/`swing` (90/50/50/160 ms), `heavy` (200/70/90/240 ms): wind-up, smear, contact, follow-through |
| `parry` | 1 | `guard` (looping hold), `cast` (hands raised) |
| `hit` | 1 | `hit`, `hurt` (flinch), first frame of `death` |
| `down2`, `down3` | 2 | `death` (kneel → on the ground, holds last) |
| **Total** | **19 × 4 = 76** | identical frame table to today |

Additions after the swap (new keys, optional, fall back via `ACTION_ANIMATIONS`): `cast1-2` (a real cast pose),
`dodge1-2` (roll), `guard` (shield up held). Add only when Thorne/Wren's combat needs them.

### 2.5 Enemies and the Crow Warden: telegraph readability
- Each enemy needs a silhouette distinct from the player and from every other enemy at 1×, at 25% screen
  brightness (night) and in greyscale.
- Enemy painters/sheets provide states `idle`, `move` (2-4 frames), `windup` (1-2 frames, the body visibly coils
  away from the target), `attack` (1-2 frames, extended toward the target), `recover`, `hurt`, `dead`. `drawEnemy`
  already adds the red pulse, lunge, `!` tell and ground telegraph; art must not use the reserved red/yellow so the
  code tint stays the signal.
- Wind-up pose is held for the whole telegraph window (`actionRules` wind-up time), never a 1-frame pop.
- **Crow Warden** (boss): 2-tile-wide silhouette (up to 96×96 frame), halberd and crows readable as separate shapes;
  each attack has its own wind-up pose (halberd raised = sweep arc, crows lifting = ranged burst) so the player
  reads the attack from the body, not only from the ground marker. The red-eye accent stays its signature.

## 3. Replacement order (playable at every step)

**Asset contract (drop-in):** a new character file registers a manifest with the **same frame size, anchor, pose
keys, facings, layer names and sheet-id templates** as `ENSO.spriteManifest.manaseed`, under a new key
(`enso`), with `classes: ['*']`. `characterFor()` already picks the manifest that lists `'*'`; when both exist the
new one wins via an explicit `ENSO.artSource` switch (one small adapter in `ui/sprites.js`, `?art=manaseed|enso`
URL override for side-by-side QA). Tiles follow the same idea: `ui/tiles-enso.js` exposes the same API as
`ENSO.tilesManaseed` (`KEY`, `PARTS` names, `layout()`, `ground`, `painters`, `overhangs`, `sprites`, `decorPass`),
so `ui/world-render.js` changes one lookup. A missing piece falls back to the Mana Seed piece, then to the painter.

Order (combat first, because the player and what they hit are on screen every second):

1. **Player body + 1 outfit + 1 hair style + weapon (sword) + 1 shield**: the full 76-frame table. Unlocks combat
   with zero Mana Seed on the player when defaults are chosen.
2. **Remaining weapon types** (axe, mace) and material recolours via palette swap.
3. **Creator breadth**: hair styles, outfits, hats as palette-keyed sheets; `options` lists rebuilt from ours. Saves
   keep indices: map old indices to the nearest new choice in the adapter.
4. **NPCs/villagers** (`person()`): automatically switch once 1-3 exist; Crow Warden NPC gets its own sheet.
5. **Ground tiles**: grass, flowers, dirt edges and corners, pebbles, water + sparkles (32 pieces).
6. **Objects**: tree, boulder, chest, wall/window/roof, floor, furniture (13 pieces).
7. **Enemy and Crow Warden sheets** (replacing painters where art improves readability; optional per enemy).
8. **Removal**: delete `assets/sprites/manaseed.js`, `assets/src/tiles/manaseed.png`, the `tiles/manaseed` packed
   entry, `ui/tiles-manaseed.js`, both generator tools, tests 46/47 (replaced by contract tests on our art); move the
   licence record to `docs/art/history/`.

## 4. Pipeline

| What | Where | Name |
|---|---|---|
| Editable source (Aseprite/PNG layers, palette) | `assets/src/art/<area>/` (`character/`, `tiles/`, `enemies/`, `ui/`) | `<subject>_<variant>_<size>.aseprite`, e.g. `body_base_64.aseprite`, `tiles_grass_16.aseprite` |
| Generation briefs and raw generator output | `assets/src/art/_gen/<date>-<subject>/` (`brief.md`, `raw/`, `notes.md`) | raw output is never shipped directly |
| Exported, reviewed sheets | `assets/src/art/export/` → `npm run pack` → `assets/packed/assets.js` (tiles) or a generated `assets/sprites/enso-*.js` (characters) | sheet ids `enso.<layer>.<style>[.<variant>]`, keys `<facing>.<pose>` |
| Master palette | `assets/src/palette/enso-master.gpl` | |
| Generator script | `tools/gen-enso-sprites.js` (Quill), mirrors `tools/gen-manaseed.js` output shape | edit the tool, never the output |

**Licence:** all new art is original, made for and owned by the ENSO project (Tokelo). Record this in
`assets/LICENSE-enso-art.txt` together with the generator used, its terms, and a statement that no third-party art
(Mana Seed or `assets/basesprite/`) was used as input or reference.

**Review of generated output** (every batch, before it enters `export/`):
1. Provenance: `brief.md` shows the prompt/inputs; inputs are only our own palette, our own earlier ENSO art and
   words. Any Mana Seed or unlicensed input → batch rejected and deleted.
2. Quill's checks (scripted): frame size, anchor row, every pixel in the master palette, no semi-transparent
   pixels, every required `<facing>.<pose>` key present, `validateSheets()` clean.
3. Saffron's art review: silhouette at 1×, style guide §2, consistency with the other layers.
4. Hand cleanup in Aseprite is expected; generated output is a draft.
5. Tally's screenshots (M-stage below) before the swap is merged.
